# Administrative mutation attribution audit and design — Phase 4B.4A

Audited: 2026-11-15. Status: design only; no attribution implementation or migration.

## Result and evidence boundaries

Phase 4A finding A04 remains confirmed: none of the 26 administrative mutation endpoints inventoried below durably records its authenticated initiator. Existing authorization is present; this is a provenance gap, not evidence that those endpoints are publicly writable. An account ID used as an employee link, department head, or team lead identifies the subject or responsible person, not the administrator who made the change.

The audit inspected the current working tree, including the uncommitted Phase 2A–4B.3 implementations, controllers, services, repositories, lifecycle helpers, Prisma schema/migrations, row-lock utilities, domain history writers, and offline test harness. References: [Phase 4A, A04](superadmin-comprehensive-authorization-audit.md#a04--actor-provenance-is-incomplete-outside-the-new-attributed-mutations), [department data security](department-data-access-security.md), [department GET integrity](department-get-integrity.md), and [Client creation permission separation](client-creation-permission-separation.md).

Only this report and its feature-index link are changed in this phase. Findings describe inspected source; proposed tables, fields, actions, and files below do not exist yet. No production code, schema, migration, or existing test is modified. No live database is queried.

## Authentication and authorization baseline

[`auth.middleware.ts`](../../apps/api/src/features/auth/auth.middleware.ts) validates the Bearer token, resolves its persisted session and current account, and rejects expired sessions, inactive accounts, and retired accounts. `request.auth.user` is the authoritative backend actor. The middleware can update session activity; that is not management attribution and must not become an HR history event.

The actual fixed-role mapping in [`auth.permissions.ts`](../../apps/api/src/features/auth/auth.permissions.ts) controls the following endpoints. Account Management is Superadmin-only, with additional permission and target-policy checks. Admin and Superadmin have employee/department management permissions. Staff, including Department Heads, have no generic HR management grant. Department Head operational permissions elsewhere remain independent. Client creation requires both `clients:read` and `clients:create`; Client update/deletion still require Superadmin. The global Phase 2A capabilities do not replace these route guards or grant operational participation.

Some platform-skill descriptions are stale: they mention an `AuditLog` with `PASSWORD_RESET` and broader Admin account-management permissions. Neither an `AuditLog` model/table nor a `PASSWORD_RESET` attribution writer exists in the current Prisma schema, checked-in migrations, or API source. Current route guards and permission catalogs, rather than those descriptions, are the implementation reference.

In the inventories, **durable attribution is required for every listed successful management operation**. “None” means the actor is not persisted, even when supplied for authorization. Denied/failed attempts are not successful domain changes and are outside this proposed history design.

There is no separate employee-record deletion or general employee restoration endpoint in this inventory. HR status and organizational assignments change through `PUT`; login retirement/restoration is a distinct account lifecycle. Do not invent new operations or conflate `Employee.status` with `User.isActive`.

`writeEmployee` can also replace/restore a Department Head, maintain temporary-original/assigned tracking, clear a departing team lead, synchronize User fields, and clear displaced head titles. These are existing consequences of the administrator's operation. None has initiating-actor history. Record their actual changed identities/statuses under the same administrator and operation ID; do not attribute them to the employee being changed, a deputy, or a fabricated system user.

Successful account permanent deletion can independently clear `Employee.userId`, `Department.headId`, temporary-head tracking IDs, and `Team.leadId` through existing `onDelete: SetNull` relations. Capture any actual affected workforce subjects before deletion and record their resulting changes within that transaction. Do not add a new prohibition or automatic replacement workflow merely to support attribution.

## Recommended minimum persistence design

Add two domain-owned append-only models in `apps/api/prisma/schema.prisma`, with no modification of existing business relationships:

| Proposed model              | Bounded purpose and subject                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `AccountManagementChange`   | User creation, role/status, administrator password reset, retirement/restoration/permanent deletion, Client profile edits, and Employee-login lifecycle. Required `subjectAccountId`              |
| `WorkforceManagementChange` | Employee, Department, and Team creation/updates/deletion, membership/leadership, and associated synchronization. Required closed `subjectType` (`EMPLOYEE`, `DEPARTMENT`, `TEAM`) and `subjectId` |

Both need `id`, required immutable `actorAccountId`, `actorRoleAtTime` using existing `Role`, a server-generated `operationId`, a closed domain-specific `action` enum, a closed origin identifying the existing management surface, database-default `createdAt`, and bounded `changedFields`/allowlisted `changes` JSON. Explicit action families are account create/profile/role/status/password-reset/archive/restore/delete and workforce create/profile/status/assignment/head/temporary-head/lead/delete. Validate each action's subject and payload; do not accept arbitrary table names, event types, or request JSON. A single row can capture multiple changed fields for one affected subject. Domain-specific enums are proposed schema additions, not new permissions.

`actorAccountId` and subject IDs should be immutable scalar snapshots **without deletion-restricting or cascading foreign keys**. This preserves actual identities after target or actor permanent deletion without changing current deletion eligibility. Do not use an actor email/name snapshot as a substitute. The tradeoff is that referential validity must be established by the authenticated application path and tested; a history row must never use an unvalidated client actor ID. Existing FK guards on business/history records stay unchanged.

Index by subject plus `(createdAt, id)`, actor plus `(createdAt, id)`, and `operationId`. Timestamp/ID order is a display order, not a promise of global commit order. No new version column or concurrency-control scheme is needed solely for attribution.

Use two small feature-local writers: proposed `users/account-management-history.ts` and `employees/workforce-management-history.ts`. They accept the caller's existing `Prisma.TransactionClient` and typed allowlisted values. Department/team services can import the workforce writer as they already import Employee synchronization. Do not introduce generic HTTP logging middleware, a universal event bus, cross-domain trigger capture, a new UI, or a public history endpoint.

### Allowed and excluded values

- Retain opaque database subject/actor IDs, actor role at authentication, action/origin, changed field names, before/after fixed roles, login/HR statuses, department/team/head/temporary-head/link IDs, and relevant lifecycle timestamps.
- For changed names, email, contact number, job-title text, employee code, descriptions, start date, and other HR/profile fields, record that the field changed without retaining its content. For title cleanup, record the affected subject, field, and closed cleanup cause; do not copy free-form titles.
- For password reset or initial credentials, record successful action and session-revocation outcome only. Never include plaintext, hashes, previous hashes, salts, tokens, refresh tokens, Authorization headers, raw request bodies, or `User`/Employee object spreads. Secret hashes/digests are not an acceptable alternative snapshot.
- Generated Employee/User synchronization is a derived effect of the root operation. Record material workforce changes, including login-link removal caused by `SetNull`, and department/team cascades; do not duplicate mirrored User profile fields as independent account operations. Legacy User-only assignment changes can be represented in workforce change data with that affected account ID and parent department/team subject.

This is provenance, not a complete HR revision archive. Reconstructing historical personal profiles, history-search UI, retention policy automation, export, and tamper-evident external storage are separate decisions. Opaque IDs and organizational assignments remain sensitive data; attribution does not authorize wider readers.

## Transaction and concurrency design

1. At each authenticated management entry point, derive the actor from `request.auth.user`. Pass only the required `Pick<User, "id" | "role">` and a server-generated operation ID separately from validated input. Never accept actor, role-at-time, operation ID, or event time from body/query/headers. Do not pass the complete authenticated User, including its credential hash, to a history writer. Keep current authorization middleware and target checks.
2. Carry that context into the existing transaction and nested domain helpers. Account services already have actor data; extend their repository calls. Client and department/team controllers must begin passing it. Employee profile writers need it in addition to the actor IDs currently used by login lifecycle self-checks. Cover direct service callers as well as HTTP handlers; no silent `null`, target-as-actor, or fake-system fallback for these administrative paths.
3. Read the necessary before-state inside the transaction with the existing locking/conditional-write rules. Use explicit safe field selects. Derive the after-state from successful writes or an in-transaction select, not the submitted body. Preserve Department → Employee → User lock ordering, sorted row acquisition, and existing isolation levels. Do not add actor row locks that reverse this order.
4. Append history before the transaction returns, after confirming the guarded mutation succeeded. For Account Management permanent deletion, introduce an enclosing transaction around the existing guarded deletion and new history insert. Other listed writers already have transactions. A failed history insert must roll back the business change and session revocations; a failed business operation must leave no success history. Do not write success records after commit or in catch/finally blocks.
5. Allocate one operation ID per backend management operation and propagate it through provisioning, employee sync, head handoff/restoration, title cleanup, bulk membership, and deletion cascades. Produce a bounded record per affected subject/material action, without duplicates for every helper/SQL update. Collect cascade subject IDs before deletion in the same transaction. Preserve all original business effects and validations.
6. Preserve current response/conflict semantics: validation errors, authorization denials, missing targets, linked-history denial, zero-row conditional conflicts, deadlocks, and serialization failures create no committed success history. Do not silently retry a management command or turn a conflict into success for attribution. If an existing transaction is retried, failed attempts and their records roll back; reuse its server operation context. An operation ID does not provide HTTP retry idempotency or exactly-once delivery.
7. For actual no-op field updates, avoid claiming a state transition. Preserve current behavior even if it updates `updatedAt` or returns success. An explicit credential reset or completed disable command that revokes sessions can still warrant an action record with truthful unchanged before/after state; do not skip revocation to suppress a duplicate-looking event. No new rejection or workflow rule is proposed.
8. Concurrent before/after values must describe the committed change under existing transaction isolation, locks, or guarded expected values. In team update, the current `existing` read occurs outside the transaction; re-read the attribution before-image inside it without changing eligibility rules. Department deletion's service/history prechecks also precede the repository transaction; do not treat those reads as atomic history snapshots. Add the minimum transactional snapshot locking needed for accurate attribution, following existing lock helpers; any broader eligibility-race redesign requires separate review. Do not expand the lock whitelist casually to introduce a new Team locking strategy. For bulk assignment/deletion, an unlocked pre-read of IDs does not prove which rows a concurrent predicate update actually changed: capture the actual affected set under tested transactional protection. If that requires a broader persistence/locking redesign, defer that path explicitly rather than recording a speculative before-image or claiming full coverage.

`actorRoleAtTime` means the authenticated backend account snapshot authorizing this request, not a client claim or a later lookup after commit. Concurrent revocation/role changes continue to follow current request-authentication semantics. Transaction-time actor reauthorization would be a separate policy change, not an implied attribution feature. Failed-attempt security telemetry is outside this domain-success history design.

## Migration and security considerations

- Use an additive migration only; no rewrite of existing account/HR rows, no synthetic historical actors, and no backfilled “Superadmin” attribution inferred from target roles or timestamps. Document the first deployment time from which coverage exists.
- Build/regenerate Prisma with `pnpm db:generate` when the future schema is changed. Apply and test the migration with the existing offline migration harness; deployment uses existing migration procedures, not an automatic repair on a GET request.
- Treat history as append-only through application code: no update/delete endpoints or mutable model exposure. This does not make records tamper-proof against a database owner. Do not claim stronger database-role protections than the deployment actually enforces; hardened retention/DB permissions are a separate reviewed decision.
- Scalar actor/subject snapshots must survive successful hard deletion. Do not add `Restrict` or cascading FKs that block today's permissible deletions or erase provenance. Test former actor deletion as well as target deletion; retain all pre-existing related-record restrictions.
- Use action-specific allowlists before serialization; never redact a full snapshot after storing it. Do not reuse Attendance whole-row trigger JSON for User data. Bounded metadata and field counts prevent accidental oversized or arbitrary client content persistence.
- A history-write failure must fail the management transaction. This adds a deliberate atomic dependency; deploy schema before application writers. Rolling back writers must leave already-written history intact. Do not automatically drop history tables to roll back application code.
- Preserve Phase 4B.1 field-level restrictions, Phase 4B.2 observational department reads, Phase 4B.3 `clients:create`, existing self/target guards, fixed roles, credential validation, session revocation, leadership eligibility, and workflow restrictions. Do not introduce a GET-triggered repair or history backfill.

## Proposed incremental implementation sequence

1. **Account foundation and credential/lifecycle operations:** approve the bounded schema/payload contract, add additive migration and two small typed writers, implement Account Management attribution with rollback/privacy tests. Include existing role/status guards and permanent-delete survival from the outset. No operational or permission changes.
2. **Client and Employee-login account paths:** reuse account history for Client create/edit/archive and Staff-login provisioning/lifecycle. Propagate the same operation into Employee creation/link/unlink so cross-domain operations cannot commit partial provenance. Keep Admin Client edits denied.
3. **Employee and leadership effects:** attribute employee profile/status/assignment changes and all existing temporary-head replacement/restoration/title-cleanup effects within their current transaction. Preserve the Phase 4B.2 write locations and read-only GET tests.
4. **Department/team structures and memberships:** attribute creation/edit/deletion, explicit head/lead changes, bulk assignment, legacy User paths, and cascade effects. Validate concurrent before-images without broader workflow refactoring; finish the full existing regression suite and coverage inventory.

Each increment must be independently reviewable and must not claim complete attribution for paths not yet wired. No implementation increment is authorized by this audit deliverable itself.
